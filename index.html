Positioning, differentiation and the execution brief

Brand direction for the A+ audience

CasaDoctors should feel composed, useful and easy to approach. A+ is the client's target direction: people for whom time, confidence and the quality of the arranging experience matter. It does not establish that every customer prefers English, wants luxury imagery or chooses the highest price. Research should confirm language, service and trust preferences. Our creative leads with a familiar problem and a credible next step, not status symbols or claims of superior medicine.

When someone in the family needs an appointment, the uncertainty is practical: who is available, where they are, and which appointment fits. The desired position is a considered local way to find and arrange care around your life. CasaDoctors brings relevant options, appointment information and booking support together so the customer has less searching and a clearer next step. The launch offer names the actual service/catchment; broader family-service ambition is introduced as categories become ready.

How we make people welcome the brand

Open with the customer's situation, not “a revolutionary healthcare ecosystem.” Show a calm, recognizable moment: finding a suitable time around work or arranging an appointment for a family member. Then demonstrate the supported choices. Use readable screens, space, restrained color and real permissioned provider material. Speak to people as capable adults; avoid dramatizing illness or suggesting something about their personal health.

Use an invitation such as “Explore available appointments,” with current service status stated plainly. Let the customer understand what is offered before asking for a commitment; any change to registration flow needs product review. Provider outreach opens with their actual available capacity and staff workflow, not a promise of unlimited patients. Customer and provider destinations, calls to action and reports stay separate.

Days 15–30: run separately authorized customer/provider awareness tests. Days 31–60: earn the claim through verified providers, accurate location/slot information, tested nearest-versus-earliest choices and demonstrated help/confirmation. Days 61–90: after pilot-entry approval, publish service-live copy and use real destinations; measure useful options, booking outcomes and whether extra calls were still needed. Live creative is not used merely because a scheduled date has arrived.

Creative tests and acceptance

Test two restrained customer routes: a practical appointment-fit headline and a family-arranging story. Test the provider availability headline against a straightforward local-network invitation. Use the same valid destination to isolate message differences; do not force identical media allocations where the eligible audiences differ.

Ask target participants what the app does, whether the message feels relevant/welcoming, what they expect after clicking and what would make them trust the offer. Keep English and Arabic comprehension results separate. Record actual response counts and comments; the existing exploratory checks are not a representative brand-lift study. Reject copy that implies unavailable services, instant care, clinical superiority or competitor shortcomings we have not tested.

In the product proof, verify the promised location/time comparison, current usable slots, confirmation and recovery with real participating providers. Nearest and earliest can be different choices; make that understandable. Provider funds, personal medical information and health-revealing data remain in approved operating systems. The marketing story neither assumes an AI diagnosis engine nor certifies an untested capability.

My reading of Vezeeta: what I want us to learn

I see three useful communication moves in this verified campaign set: start from a recognizable Egyptian behavior, make the digital service feel understandable, and introduce a later product through one concrete benefit. I am using these as creative lessons for CasaDoctors, not as evidence that copying the executions would reproduce Vezeeta’s growth. These portfolio entries do not establish its original launch sequence, media spend or commercial success.

90 Million Doctors: turn a cultural truth into an acquisition idea

What Vezeeta did: built several symptom-themed executions around the familiar habit of people offering medical opinions. Kairo states app downloads as the objective. My reading is that the relatable situation gives the viewer a reason to pay attention before the product asks for an action. The agency verifies the campaign idea and objective; it does not publish the platform mix, acquisition cost or resulting bookings. Kairo campaign reference ↗.

What I will apply: our insight is the effort of arranging a suitable nearby appointment for someone we love. I will open with that moment, then show the practical details people need—place, time and confirmation—and one clear next step. I will keep the warmth and specificity, while using CasaDoctors’ selected family message. We measure comprehension and exposure first, then actual first completed services after readiness; installs alone will not be our success story.

The Future of Health Care Is Now: give the service a bigger frame

What is verified: Kairo lists the campaign title and a video, with a November 2017 portfolio entry. The proposition invites people to see digital healthcare as contemporary and accessible; that is my interpretation of the framing, not a published objective or result. Kairo campaign reference ↗.

What I will apply: I will explain how the experience works rather than lead with technology jargon. For customers we demonstrate nearby options, a suitable time and a clear next step. For providers we demonstrate how an available slot becomes easier to discover and how reception handles confirmation. That gives the digital promise a practical meaning; the current app build must support the demonstration.

Commercial Model and Provider Offer

Read commercial model and provider offer

The commercial task is to negotiate a viable category offer before promoting it. Opening proposals are 15% on fulfilled eligible clinic consultation fees and 20% for coordinated home consultations only where the extra work/cost is supported. Up to 25% requires an evidenced managed-service offer; it is not a default rate. The client's original 15–25% range is a negotiation basis, not a signed category tariff. Do not apply those percentages to medicine baskets without margin and qualified review.

Define the fee basis, exclusions, discounts, tax treatment, failed/cancelled service, refund funding, provider payout and disputes in the term sheet. Weekly reconciled payout is an operating proposal subject to processor rules and signed terms. Client-confirmed in-app payment availability is accepted; the proof phase tests the complete deployed financial workflow.

Founding-provider proposal: at most ten selected providers; zero CasaDoctors commission for 30 calendar days of bookable availability or ten fulfilled eligible services, whichever comes first. Sign the ongoing rate before activation. Record per-provider start, expiry, eligible completions, waived commission and variable costs. No patient-volume guarantee or silent extension.

For every selected category, obtain actual eligible fee, signed commission, processing/refund/support and fulfillment cost. Unit contribution = earned commission minus platform variable costs. Media-only first-completion CAC = relevant acquisition spend divided by attributed first-time completed customers; fully loaded acquisition cost is reported separately. Proposed paid-test recovery horizon and acceptable loss limits need sponsor agreement using actual evidence. No repeat healthcare demand is assumed merely to improve a model.

Vireal owns delivery; each phase specifies the outputs, dates and acceptance evidence. Commercial validation uses actual signed provider terms, costs and completed-service results.
