01

Care where life happens

My recommendation: build a home-service network people can use for themselves, their family or their pets. Make appropriate video consultations the second priority.

Son abroad · books and pays → Mother in Egypt · receives care ← Approved local provider
Proposed journey. A booking from abroad does not create provider coverage abroad.

For customers

Arrange care at the address that matters, for yourself or someone you are authorized to help.

For providers

Choose suitable services, areas and hours; review requests you can genuinely fulfill.

A son is in the United States. His mother is in Egypt. She needs a suitable consultation, but arranging the visit means coordinating the right professional, her address, a time she can receive them and the payment. The son wants to help from where he is. His mother needs a service that respects her choices and privacy.

I want CasaDoctors to make that coordination clearer. The customer chooses who needs the service and where it should happen. A qualified provider makes suitable services available within the area and hours they can actually cover. Booking and payment happen through a tested app journey; delivery happens at the supported address, or by an appropriate video consultation.

This is the strategic direction, not a claim that every step is already built. We will first prove a small home-doctor service, then add video and other categories when their operating requirements are ready.

Our two-sided proposition

Customers arrange care where they need it. Providers choose when they are available to deliver it.

Our first 90 days will prepare the channels, recruit and activate providers, repair product gaps and run a bounded home-service pilot. The year then deepens what works, adds separately approved categories and considers an adjacent area. I am not recommending an all-category national launch.

What we can claim now

Our chosen proposition is home-first care coordination with provider-controlled availability. We cannot claim exclusivity, superior app UX, faster arrival, larger supply or lower prices without a like-for-like test.

Build useful saved details, trusted providers, education and referrals that make the platform remembered when needed. The frequency and willingness to switch remain research questions.

04

Service priorities and launch geography

One platform can have several categories. Each category still needs a complete operating service.

Healthcare at home · First pilot · D61–90

Start with scheduled, non-emergency adult doctor visits in one supported area. This directly serves adults arranging care for parents and people who find travel difficult. A narrow service lets us prove travel, acceptance and follow-through.

A clinician-reviewed list of suitable adult consultations delivered by qualified providers. Nursing follows as a separately approved task-based service; it is not automatically included in a doctor visit.

Before release: Credentials and scope; appropriate case screening; service address; travel buffers; defined visit duration; clinical documentation route; fee inclusions; home-visit safety and support; tested payment and refund flow.

Outside scope: Emergency response, hospital-level care, guaranteed diagnosis, unrestricted procedures, continuous bedside care or 24/7 coverage.

Video consultations · Second priority · late pilot / M4

Useful when the professional judges remote care appropriate. It can help someone arrange a suitable consultation without a journey, but it cannot stand in for every physical examination.

Start with one approved human-care specialty/use case after the home service is stable. Veterinary video is a separate professional scope and release decision.

Before release: Recipient identity and physical location; consent; clinical suitability; secure room access; time zones; connection test; failed-call recovery; documentation; local authorization and refund terms.

Outside scope: Automatic substitution of video for an unavailable home visit, remote prescribing promises, recording by default or unrestricted cross-border clinical practice.

Veterinary services at home · Separate local pilot · M4 onward

Pet owners have a distinct travel and handling problem. Build a recognizable pet-care path rather than placing a pet inside a human patient form.

Suitable consultations, vaccinations and treatments for supported species and cases, determined by the veterinarian and approved service catalogue.

Before release: Veterinary credentials; species and handling information; service area; equipment and vaccine cold-chain arrangements where relevant; safe-home conditions; escalation and separate records.

Outside scope: Emergency animal transport, every species/procedure, guaranteed treatment at home or a human medical provider handling animal care.

Home diagnostics · Contracted partner pilot · M5–6+

A useful next step for households coordinating care, provided collection, transport, results and exceptions have an accountable operator.

Eligible sample collection and explicitly approved mobile diagnostics through participating providers. A scan requiring fixed equipment stays outside the home offer.

Before release: Signed partner; test catalogue; preparation instructions; collection slots; sample identification and transport; rejected-sample recovery; result access controlled by consent; critical-result clinical escalation.

Outside scope: Naming El Mokhtabar or Al Borg as partners, offering every test at home or sending results automatically to the person who paid.

Pharmacy delivery · Contracted local pilot · M5–6+

Helpful alongside care, but inventory, pharmacist review and delivery introduce a different operating model. Add it when a participating pharmacy can fulfill reliably.

Eligible pharmacy products from a verified participating pharmacy, with required prescription/pharmacist checks and an approved delivery area.

Before release: Seller identity; prescription route where required; inventory/price confirmation; substitutions only with approval; delivery fee/window; packaging/temperature control; failed delivery; approved returns/refunds.

Outside scope: Unrestricted medicine access, automatic prescription generation, delivery within a fixed number of minutes or guaranteed stock.

Relevant care products · Evaluate after core proof · M7+

Keep commerce attached to a real care need. A general marketplace would dilute the proposition and introduce fulfillment work before the care service is proven.

Potentially approved mobility aids, basic home-care supplies or pet-care essentials from accountable sellers. Product suitability and permitted sale require review.

Before release: Curated catalogue; seller of record; product claims; stock; warranty/return rules; delivery and refund responsibility; category-specific signed terms.

Outside scope: Unrelated electronics/fashion, own inventory by default, unverified health claims, automatic baskets or compulsory products bundled with a consultation.

My first offer is scheduled, non-emergency adult home-doctor consultations in a researched Fifth Settlement / New Cairo cluster anchored around North Lotus. Use an approximately 5 km starting catchment for research, then define actual coverage by travel time, provider starting location, access and service windows. A radius on a map is not a delivery promise.

Recommend an initial proposed booking-support window of Sunday–Thursday, 10:00–18:00 Cairo, with visits finishing inside an actually covered operating period. Provider evening availability is worth researching, but we will not advertise it until corresponding acceptance, support, travel and escalation coverage is arranged. The calendar examples are not approved working hours.

Video is second priority: prepare it during the home pilot and release a small approved cohort no earlier than D75 if its own checks pass; otherwise hold it into M4. For the first remote-care release, keep the recipient physically in the approved Egypt jurisdiction. A relative can book from abroad, but that does not authorize the clinician to practice across borders.

A veterinary home pilot can follow in M4 if separately ready. Nursing is task-based and needs its own professional scope. In M5–6, consider diagnostics or pharmacy delivery one at a time after operator agreements and recovery tests. Care-product commerce is a later decision, not a condition for launch.

Booking country ≠ service country

For the US–Egypt story, the booker may be in the US, while the professional and recipient are in the approved Egypt service area. Access, OTP, international cards, currencies and terms still need verification. No worldwide provider network or global clinical license is implied.

International access is a product ambition; international fulfillment is a separate business launch.

The earlier A+ emphasis becomes a service-quality standard, rather than a claim that only affluent customers need home care. Older adults, people with limited mobility, local caregivers and relatives abroad have different priorities and purchasing power. We will test practical willingness to pay, trust and assisted-booking needs in the first area.

The initial proposed research sample is 12 adults/caregivers, including at least four arranging care for someone else and, where reachable, four living outside the recipient’s city/country; groups may overlap. Include recipient voices where appropriate and eight relevant providers. These are exploratory samples, not representative market statistics. Ask for their last real experience before showing our concept.

Test: who made the decision, who consented, who paid, which address mattered, what home service was appropriate, how long coordination took, what alternatives they trusted and what made the fee acceptable. Ask providers about actual evening capacity, travel, unsuitable cases and cancellations. Log objections and use them to revise the offer at D14.

Design requirement, not a completed feature list

The interactive journey selector shows the intended experience and the checks needed before release. No live booking, payment or clinical consultation is performed here.

A customer must be able to tell the difference between a request, acceptance, payment and confirmation. I recommend reserving capacity, authorizing payment and capturing after provider acceptance where the payment partner supports it. If the existing system instead collects before acceptance, it must explicitly show pending status and provide tested rejection/refund handling. Finance and technical owners choose one supported flow before pilot.

A provider who rejects or does not respond within the agreed request window does not create a confirmed appointment. Release the slot hold and recover payment under the approved policy. Offer alternatives with customer agreement. Do not silently substitute the professional, service mode, pet/human service or service address.

On completion, the professional records the service, finance reconciles the eligible transaction and the authorized customer receives a receipt and next-step information. Clinical records and diagnostic results go through the approved private route, with permissions independent of payment.

Providers: useful time, realistic commitments

Make your available time useful. Choose when you work and receive relevant requests within the area and services you support.

Provider availability · illustrative capacity, not live inventory

An available evening needs room for the road.

Each slot reserves visit + buffer time. This intentionally conservative example is a planning model; actual journeys and routing may differ. Evening service stays unpublished until support, provider and escalation coverage are accepted.

The promise to a provider is control and relevance, not guaranteed bookings or earnings. An evening opening only helps if the professional can travel, deliver the approved service and get support during that window. CasaBusiness should make those constraints explicit.

Recruit individual qualified doctors, eligible home-care professionals and veterinarians as professional tracks. Recruit pharmacies, labs, diagnostic businesses and suitable merchants as operating-business tracks. Ownership, permitted service scope, staff verification, fulfillment responsibility and commercial terms differ. A single generic signup form is not enough.
